# CFO proposal: a lean, fundable Cgate

## Summary
The current Base plan is not investable. It needs about £1.96m to reach about £1.14m ARR, has no profitable year by 2031 and returns about 0.9x to a seed investor. Most of the gap comes from building full vet practice software. My lean AI-first plan drops that build, has no more than 4.5 paid developers and sales people in any year before 2031, and launches studs in 2027 as hosted installs. It reaches the same ARR (£1.12m) with **£0.88m** of funding and **turns profitable in 2030**. Add the founder's contacts and it needs **£0.66m** (cash low point £327k) and **turns profitable in 2029**. I recommend raising **£450k pre-seed (SEIS + EIS angels) in early 2027**. A second £250k–£400k should follow in 2028 only if milestones are met, as revenue-based finance or EIS. The founder keeps about **66–72%**. The honest limit: even the lean plan is a capital-efficient, profitable niche business worth about £4–7m by 2031. It is not a 10x venture case. Growth slows to about 30% a year by 2031. Getting to Series A needs a second engine, such as Ireland, syndicate owner apps, payments or vet software funded from profits.

- **SEIS:** a company can raise up to £250k. It must have gross assets of £350k or less, fewer than 25 employees and under 3 years of trading. Investors get 50% income tax relief, up to £200k a year ([British Business Bank](https://www.british-business-bank.co.uk/business-guidance/guidance-articles/finance/what-is-the-seed-enterprise-investment-scheme-seis), [Taylor Wessing](https://www.taylorwessing.com/-/media/taylor-wessing/files/uk/2208_brocure_seed-enterprise-investment-scheme-seis.pdf)).
- **EIS:** investors get 30% relief. From 6 April 2026 the company limit is £10m a year and £24m lifetime ([Osborne Clarke](https://www.osborneclarke.com/insights/uk-autumn-budget-2025-what-tax-measures-were-announced), [gov.uk changes page](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/changes-to-the-seed-enterprise-investment-scheme-enterprise-investment-scheme-and-venture-capital-trusts/changes-to-venture-capital-schemes)). Get SEIS/EIS advance assurance from HMRC before pitching.
- **Founder ownership:** about 72% after the pre-seed and a 10% option pool. About 66% if step 2 is equity. For comparison, the Base plan (£1.96m at £3m pre-money) leaves about 54%.
- **Pre-seed investor return (my calculation, scenario c):** about 1.7x at a 4x multiple and about 2.6x at 6x. SEIS relief roughly doubles the return on the SEIS portion. A 10x return needs about £27m of value, or about £4.5m ARR. That is about 4x this plan.

**Caveats:**
- These gross margins leave out support staff. A realistic margin is 80–85%.
- The race price (£7.60 per horse) is about 5x Yardman's £1.50 per horse. If that does not hold, the price-cut scenario costs about 11% of ARR.

**Which bundles to sell:**
- **Sell first:** race yards (paid set-up fee, low churn, owner updates) and studs (built today, weakest competition).
- **Next:** Yard + Field, once self-serve exists.
- **Yard only:** self-serve only, never sold by a person.
- **Farrier:** free or cheap, to bring in yards. Do not plan on it as a profit line.
- **Vet practice software:** only from profits or with a funded partner.

## 5. Valuation logic
- The median private B2B SaaS company is valued at about 4.8x ARR if bootstrapped and 5.3x if equity-backed ([SaaS Capital](https://www.saas-capital.com/blog-posts/private-saas-company-valuations-multiples/)). Below £1m ARR, buyers often value on profit instead.
- **Credible multiple for Cgate: 3–4x now, 4–5x by 2031** (about £4.7m–£5.9m in plan c).
- What would raise it to 6x or more:
  - Net revenue retention above 105%, from module upsell and per-owner pricing for syndicates.
  - Livery churn below 20%.
  - Growth held above 40%, for example by expanding to Ireland.
  - Payments revenue (about 1.5% of revenue today).
  - Profitability.
  - Proof that the horse identity ledger or marketplaces work.

## 6. Financial risks and cash rules
**Risks:**
1. **Sales speed.** At Low speed, ARR is £0.70m and funding needed is £0.94m.
2. **Pricing pressure.** Competitors charge much less.
3. **A shrinking trainer base.** There are 553 licensed trainers now, down from 666.
4. **Livery churn** of 25–30%.
5. **Infrastructure not built.** Multi-tenant, billing and sign-up do not exist yet, and hosted installs carry support cost.
6. **AI productivity.** If AI does not deliver, 1.5x the developers adds about £0.15m–£0.2m a year.
7. **Key person.** Everything rests on one founder.
8. **Customer concentration.** There is one paying client today.

**Cash rules:**
- Keep at least 12 months of runway. Start raising at 9 months left.
- Hire a developer or sales person only when:
  - net new ARR over the last 2 quarters is at least 1.5x that hire's yearly cost,
  - CAC payback on the last 20 deals is below 12 months, and
  - sales capacity used is above 90%.
- Keep the burn multiple (net burn ÷ net new ARR) below 2.
- No spend on vet practice software until the company is profitable for 2 quarters, or a partner funds it.
- Cap hosted installs at 15 until multi-tenant ships.
- Charge set-up fees. Offer annual prepayment at about 10% off.
- Keep AI tooling below 5% of revenue once revenue passes £300k.
- Review Low-speed cash monthly.

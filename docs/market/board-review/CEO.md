# CEO proposal: Cgate strategy

## Summary

As planned today, Cgate is a good small business but not a venture-scale one. The UK market we can reach is about £10m to £12m a year (model, Bundles sheet). Even with no vet build and a much smaller AI-first team, my test of the model still shows about £1.05m ARR in 2031 and a seed return of about 1.1x to 1.7x. Seed funds want 10x. So the plan is this. Win the **thoroughbred "between races" segment** first: studs, pre-training yards and grazing or spelling yards that care for racehorses. The product already fits them, they pay the most per horse, and the founder knows them. Bring in **five racing trainers as paid design partners**, because trainers send horses to those yards and each one pulls more yards in. Raise a **small SEIS/EIS angel round (£300k to £400k) now**, not £2m. Stop work on vet practice software, marketplaces and self-serve small livery. Hit clear proof points by month 24 (about 110 accounts, about £250k ARR, low churn, horses moving between customers). Then choose between two paths: raise a real seed round to take the model to Ireland and other thoroughbred markets, or stay a profitable, lean company. The investor story is not "UK yard software". It is **the working record of the thoroughbred's life, kept by every business that handles the horse**, with payments running through it.

## 1. The wedge: thoroughbred studs and grazing or spelling yards, with trainers as design partners

| Segment | Fit today | Price paid per account (model) | Competition | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Studs, pre-training, grazing and spelling yards | Built: breeding, fields, rest, daily rates, invoices, Xero | £1,230 to £2,520 a year | Few tools | **Wedge** |
| Racing trainers (about 553, falling) | Race-training module not built | about £3,630 a year | miStable, Yardman, TLore | 5 paid design partners only |
| Small grass and DIY livery | Fits, but needs self-serve | about £490 a year | Crowded; free tiers exist | Postpone |
| Vets | C-vet trial only (Tim) | £4,320 a year | Provet, Covetrus, ezyVet | Free side app only |

Why: the product was built for exactly this kind of business, and it shows (score 3.35, ranked 2nd on the Investability sheet). The founder's contacts turn sales into warm introductions, not cold calls. Most important, racehorses move between stud, pre-trainer, trainer and spelling yard. Every trainer customer can invite the yards they send horses to. That is our only real chance of a network effect. Early trainers get a hosted install (£750 set-up), selling **owner updates** first because that module is cheap to build. We do not build a full race-training module until 5 trainers have paid.

## 2. Long-term position and the investor story

**Position:** the working record of a horse's life, kept by the businesses that care for it (stud, yard, trainer, vet, farrier). The record moves with the horse when it changes yard, and payments run on the invoices. We call this the identity ledger in one sentence; it is not a separate product. Defra's Central Equine Database is admitted to be "inaccurate and incomplete", and passports are still on paper ([gov.uk](https://gov.uk/government/consultations/improvements-to-equine-identification-and-traceability-in-england/outcome/improvements-to-equine-identification-and-traceability-in-england-consultation-results-and-government-response)). A working record kept by the businesses themselves fills that gap without waiting for the government.

To be honest about the numbers (my estimate): a 10x return on a £400k round at £1.6m pre-money needs a company worth about £20m. At 4x to 5x ARR, that means about £4m to £5m ARR. That is about four times the UK plan. It needs Ireland, at least one more thoroughbred market (Australia or the US), and payments. If the month-24 proof does not show a path to that, we do not raise venture money. We stay profitable.

## 5. The three biggest risks

| Risk | How we reduce it |
|---|---|
| **The market is too small to pay back investors** (model: 0.9x) | Raise small and in stages. Test Ireland early. Run the business to break even. Only raise a seed round when the record shows horses moving between customers. |
| **Selling depends on the founder** (warm introductions run out at about 50 accounts) | Turn design partners into referrers. Work with NTF, ROA and the TBA as channels. Use horse transfers as the invitation route. Measure the share of new accounts the founder did not bring in, every month. |
| **We spread too thin** (vet, farrier, racing, livery, marketplaces all at once) | One segment. One build list. Everything else waits until its restart rule in section 3 is met. The board reviews this every quarter. |

## 6. What being AI-first changes

- **Team:** 2027: the founder, one paid co-founder or senior developer, and one person for customer success and sales. 2028: add one developer and one sales person. AI agents write and test code, import data, handle first-line support, write help content and do sales admin.
- **Cost:** in my test copy of the model (`ceo_ai_novet_cac.xlsx`: no vet developers, 1 to 2 core developers, 1 to 3 sales and support staff, lower cost to win customers through contacts), the money needed falls from about **£1.96m to about £0.92m**, and the first profitable year moves from after 2031 to **2030**. ARR stays about £1.05m. AI cuts cost; it does not grow the market.
- **Speed:** the multi-tenant and billing build goes from 4 to 6 months to about 3 (our estimate). The weak point is sales capacity: the test uses about 205% of it. AI must take on the admin so each sales person can win about 100 accounts a year, not 60. If not, we hire one more sales person in 2028.
- **Limits:** AI does not replace trust in racing. The founder still opens every first conversation, and a person still signs off every vet or medicine record.
